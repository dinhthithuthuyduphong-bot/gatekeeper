## Consensus and adjudication

All reviewers credit the large measured Jinja/Bleve gains, history controls, instruction ablation, second evaluation seed, and pinned-scorer audit. The paper explicitly recognizes prior leak reports and does not claim the announcement's numbers came from the inspected scorer. The control experiments are a plausible EASE contribution in their own right.

R1 believes that contribution can support acceptance after revisions. R2 identifies three sharper defects: the difference between a realized-pool guarantee and expected skill risk, the described concatenation of the 70-task stream, and the universally quantified no-transfer proposition defeated by a never-admit procedure. The meta-review treats these as issues to repair before publication, not reasons to dismiss the empirical effects as false. R3's confounding concerns further limit claims about the optimizer's general behavior. R4's missing registration links prevent independent assessment of the confirmatory chronology.

The priority is to settle what is certified and under which randomization, then simplify the narrative around the learned history dependency. A new leak-free experiment with legitimate history retained would strengthen attribution, but it is not a substitute for correcting the theorem application.
